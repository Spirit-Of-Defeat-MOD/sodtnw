# Spirit of Defeat: The New War

*A nation that cannot forget defeat may be more dangerous than one that has won.*

## The story so far

The Great War ended with Germany on the losing side. The Treaty of Versailles took territory, imposed reparations, and left the country with a military shackled by treaty. The Weimar Republic could not quiet the street fighting or convince its enemies that it was legitimate. In 1923, General Hans von Seeckt seized power in the Schutzputsch. The republic gave way to an authoritarian order—and the wounds of defeat were made into the foundations of a new state.

Thirteen years later, that state is still standing, but it is far from secure. The Reichswehr plans for wars it is not yet permitted to fight, while the Black Reichswehr and other paramilitaries turn political conflict into a daily threat. Reparations and unrest have crippled an already fragile economy. Seeckt has waited for the moment to reclaim Germany’s lost position; now, every decision risks bringing that moment closer.

Beyond Germany, the old certainties have also broken apart. A communist France faces an authoritarian Reich across the Rhine. The Soviet Union is a possible partner against Poland as well as a power with its own designs. Italy’s fascist empire holds Croatia in its orbit, while the United States and Britain remain among the major democratic powers. Across China and Africa, a patchwork of governments and regional states faces a world whose crises will not stay local.

The next war is not inevitable—but the peace is built on grievances, rival ambitions, and compromises that may not hold. Germany can try to bind the country together, bargain with its workers, seek allies, rebuild its strength, or reach for a different political settlement. Whatever course it takes, the choices of 1936 will decide what the next war is fought for.
